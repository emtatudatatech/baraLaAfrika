Each record's `animal_status` was assigned by these rules, so the app never presents an inference as an official fact:

- **Inferred**: the document says the animal is inferred or that no national animal is codified.
- **De facto**: the document says 'de facto', or presents the animal as the national animal without tying it to a state emblem, a legal act or an official statement.
- **Sports nickname**: the document's only stated or cited basis is a national football team nickname (including when its sole citation is the CAF nicknames list, ref 19).
- **Official**: the document ties the animal to a state emblem (coat of arms, seal or flag) or calls it official.
- **Contested**: the record lists two or more animals with different bases, or the document's stated basis looks wrong.

## 8. Extraction notes

- Prose is read span by span: superscript citation numerals are separate spans, so a year followed by a citation (e.g. “1525” plus ref 4) is never merged into “15254”.
- Table cells that the PDF wrapped mid-word (e.g. “Red/Black/Gre|en”, “Guinea-Bissa|u”) are re-joined by a geometry rule (a single token that fills the cell width); the West Africa table's Niger row, split across pages 8–9, is merged.
- Each work cited's URL, rebuilt from the text, must exactly match one of the PDF's link annotations.
- Country names are normalised to their canonical forms with diacritics (e.g. the table's “Sao Tome” becomes São Tomé and Príncipe, “Gambia” becomes The Gambia); the prose and table text are otherwise kept as printed.
- Profile prose is split into fields (etymology, flag, symbol, animal) by a reviewed sentence mapping in `reference/symbology_curation.json`; a sentence can serve more than one field (e.g. Uganda's crowned crane).
- `is_microstate_marker` and the country centroids are set later by the boundaries pipeline; `regions.color_hex` by the flags pipeline.
